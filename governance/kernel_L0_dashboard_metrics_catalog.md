## 1. Cash & Liquidity

### 1.1 Total Cash

**What it shows**
How much money the company has immediately available.

**What it uses**
Operating Cash and Undeposited Funds.

**Why it matters**
This is survival. Everything else is secondary.

**How to read it**

* Rising: cash is being generated or injected.
* Falling: operations, investments, or obligations are consuming cash.

**Red flags**

* Strong profit but shrinking cash.
* Large swings without a clear operational reason.

**Typical visual**
Line chart over time.

**Tier**
*1*
---

### 1.2 Net Working Capital

**What it shows**
Whether short-term assets are enough to cover short-term obligations.

**What it uses**
Cash, receivables, prepaids versus payables, accrued liabilities, taxes payable, deferred revenue.

**Why it matters**
This is the company’s breathing room.

**How to read it**

* Positive and stable: healthy operations.
* Negative or shrinking: liquidity stress.

**Red flags**

* Growing revenue with declining working capital.
* Heavy reliance on delaying payments.

**Typical visual**
Bar chart by month + trend indicator.

---

### 1.3 Current Ratio (Kernel Approximation)

**What it shows**
Ability to pay short-term obligations with short-term resources.

**Why it matters**
A fast signal of liquidity risk.

**How to read it**

* Above 1: generally safe.
* Below 1: potential cash pressure.

**Limitations**
This is a simplified version. Still reliable for services and SaaS.

---

### 1.4 Quick Ratio (Kernel Approximation)

**What it shows**
Liquidity excluding prepaids.

**Why it matters**
Shows how liquid the company really is, without relying on future benefits.

**Red flags**
High current ratio but weak quick ratio → liquidity illusion.

---

## 2. Revenue & Profitability

### 2.1 Net Revenue

**What it shows**
Actual earned revenue after refunds and allowances.

**Why it matters**
This is the real top line.

**How to read it**

* Growth matters, but quality matters more.

**Red flags**
High gross revenue with high refunds.

---

### 2.2 Gross Profit

**What it shows**
Money left after direct costs.

**Why it matters**
This funds operations, payroll, and growth.

**How to read it**

* Stable margin = pricing discipline.
* Falling margin = cost pressure or pricing issues.

---

### 2.3 Gross Margin

**What it shows**
Efficiency of the core business model.

**Why it matters**
Margins tell you whether growth helps or hurts.

**Red flags**
Revenue growth with margin erosion.

---

### 2.4 Operating Income

**What it shows**
Profit from operations before financing and taxes.

**Why it matters**
This is operational truth.

**How to read it**

* Positive and growing: scalable operations.
* Volatile: cost structure instability.

---

### 2.5 Operating Margin

**What it shows**
Operational efficiency as a percentage of revenue.

**Red flags**
High payroll or overhead growth without revenue growth.

---

## 3. Cost Structure

### 3.1 Payroll Ratio

**What it shows**
How much revenue is consumed by people.

**Why it matters**
Payroll is sticky. It defines burn rate.

**How to read it**

* Stable: controlled growth.
* Rising faster than revenue: future layoffs or cash strain.

---

### 3.2 Operating Expense Ratio

**What it shows**
Total operational cost pressure.

**Why it matters**
Shows whether the company can scale.

---

## 4. Receivables, Payables & Cash Cycle

### 4.1 Days Sales Outstanding (DSO)

**What it shows**
How long the company takes to collect revenue.

**Why it matters**
Revenue is meaningless until collected.

**How to read it**

* Falling DSO: improving cash discipline.
* Rising DSO: cash strain ahead.

**Red flags**
Revenue growth with rising DSO.

---

### 4.2 Days Payable Outstanding (DPO)

**What it shows**
How long the company takes to pay its obligations.

**Why it matters**
This is cash management — or desperation.

**How to read it**

* Moderate increase: optimization.
* Excessive increase: vendor risk.

---

### 4.3 Cash Conversion Cycle (Kernel Version)

**What it shows**
How long cash is tied up in operations.

**Why it matters**
This is the heartbeat of the company.

**How to read it**
Shorter cycle = healthier cash dynamics.

---

## 5. Earnings Quality (Critical Section)

### 5.1 Operating Cash Flow (Estimated)

**What it shows**
Cash generated by operations, adjusted for accruals.

**Why it matters**
Cash exposes accounting illusions.

**How to read it**

* Close to net income: high quality earnings.
* Much lower than net income: accrual-driven profit.

---

### 5.2 Earnings Quality Ratio

**What it shows**
Cash generated per unit of profit.

**Why it matters**
This detects fragile businesses early.

**Red flags**

* Ratio below 1 for extended periods.
* Profit growth without cash growth.

---

## 6. Taxes & Fiscal Pressure

### 6.1 Effective Tax Rate (Approximate)

**What it shows**
Tax burden relative to profit.

**Why it matters**
Sustainability and planning.

---

### 6.2 Tax Coverage Ratio

**What it shows**
Ability to pay taxes with current cash.

**Red flags**
Cash insufficient to cover taxes payable.

---

### 6.3 Tax Drift Indicator

**What it shows**
Mismatch between tax expense and tax payable.

**Why it matters**
Detects deferred tax risk or payment stress.

---

## 7. Fixed Assets & Long-Term Health

### 7.1 Net Fixed Assets

**What it shows**
Productive assets after depreciation.

**Why it matters**
Shows capital intensity.

---

### 7.2 Depreciation Intensity

**What it shows**
Asset wear relative to asset base.

**Red flags**
High depreciation without reinvestment.

---

### 7.3 Asset Intensity Ratio

**What it shows**
How much asset is required to generate revenue.

**Why it matters**
Indicates scalability.

---

## 8. Projections & Scenarios

### 8.1 Revenue Growth Projection

**What it shows**
Expected revenue under selected growth assumptions.

---

### 8.2 Cost Sensitivity Scenarios

**What it shows**
How payroll, costs, and taxes react to revenue changes.

---

### 8.3 Cash Stress Simulation

**What it shows**
Impact of slower collections or faster payments on cash.

**Why it matters**
This is where companies break.
